# AI-7 — AI Evaluation

An evaluation framework for measuring retrieval quality in a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system. This project uses a manually reviewed benchmark, automated retrieval metrics, controlled Top-K experiments, and a cross-encoder reranking experiment to understand how well relevant evidence is retrieved.

> **Scope note:** The experiments documented here evaluate retrieval ranking. They do not evaluate the correctness, faithfulness, or overall quality of answers generated by a language model.

## Project goals

- Maintain a reusable benchmark of question-to-relevant-chunk examples.
- Measure retrieval performance with Precision@K, Recall@K, Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR), source hit rate, and duplicate rate.
- Compare different retrieval cutoffs without changing the underlying vector database.
- Test whether a cross-encoder can improve the ordering of retrieved candidates.
- Record failure cases, experimental reports, and limitations so results are reproducible and interpretable.

## Current implementation

- **Embedding model:** `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2`
- **Embedding dimension:** 384
- **Vector database:** ChromaDB
- **Cross-encoder reranker:** `cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`

## Evaluation methodology

### Benchmark and relevance labels

The audited benchmark contains 25 questions. Each case has an ID, question, expected answer, relevant source information, relevant chunk IDs, and a status. Benchmark validation checks that IDs are unique, questions and expected answers are nonempty, source/chunk label fields are lists of nonempty strings, and labelled cases contain at least one relevant chunk ID.

Relevant chunk labels were manually inspected for the cases discussed in the project notes. Two cases were corrected after review:

- **`rag-003`:** both `rag_reference.md::chunk_0` and `rag_reference.md::chunk_1` contain useful information about chunking and embeddings.
- **`rag-005`:** the reference answer and two overlapping documentation chunks (`rag_documentation.md::chunk_5` and `rag_documentation.md::chunk_6`) all contain relevant information about updating the index without retraining the language model.

These are manual judgments, not an independent annotation study. Scores therefore depend on the quality and completeness of the relevance labels.

### Metrics

- **Precision@K:** fraction of the first K retrieved chunks labelled relevant.
- **Recall@K:** fraction of labelled relevant chunks recovered in the first K results.
- **MRR (Mean Reciprocal Rank):** average reciprocal rank of the first relevant result.
- **Source hit rate:** fraction of questions for which a retrieved result matches a labelled relevant source.
- **Duplicate rate:** fraction of retrieved IDs that are duplicates within a result list.

Relevance metrics are averaged over labelled benchmark cases. The reported metrics measure retrieval, not generated-answer quality.

## Experiment 1 — Top-K comparison

All three runs used the same audited benchmark and existing ChromaDB collection.

| Metric | K=3 | K=5 | K=10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Precision@K | 0.440 | 0.264 | 0.136 |
| Recall@K | 0.980 | 0.980 | 1.000 |
| MRR | 0.980 | 0.980 | 0.980 |
| Source hit rate | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| Duplicate rate | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 |

### Interpretation

- K=3 returned a more concentrated set of relevant chunks, with higher precision than K=5 or K=10.
- K=10 achieved full aggregate recall, but precision fell because more retrieved chunks were not labelled relevant.
- K=3 is a candidate when concise context is important; K=10 may be preferable when recovering every labelled relevant chunk is the priority.
- These results do not establish a universal optimum. The appropriate cutoff depends on the application and the trade-off between context coverage and irrelevant material.

## Experiment 2 — Cross-encoder reranking

**Configuration**

- Candidate retrieval: Top-10 from the existing embedding retriever.
- Reranking model: `cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`.
- Final evaluation cutoff: Top-3.
- Benchmark: `data/benchmark_audited_v1.json`.
- Vector database: existing local ChromaDB collection; no re-ingestion performed.

### Aggregate results

| Metric | Direct Top-3 baseline | Reranked Top-3 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Precision@3 | 0.440 | 0.440 |
| Recall@3 | 0.980 | 0.980 |
| MRR | 0.980 | 1.000 |
| Source hit rate | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| Duplicate rate | 0.000 | 0.000 |

The reranker improved aggregate MRR by 0.020, while aggregate precision and recall remained unchanged. This is a limited aggregate improvement rather than evidence that every retrieval metric improved. Cross-encoder scores are ranking scores, not calibrated probabilities.

## Failure analysis and case studies

### Case 1 — `rag-003`: chunking and embeddings

**Question:** Why are chunking and embeddings important in RAG?

Before reranking, direct Top-3 retrieval returned one relevant chunk and two weakly related implementation chunks. Precision@3 was 0.333 and Recall@3 was 0.500.

The cross-encoder moved both labelled relevant chunks into the Top-3:
1. `rag_reference.md::chunk_1`
2. `rag_reference.md::chunk_0`
3. `rag_documentation.md::chunk_14`

After reranking, Precision@3 was 0.667 and Recall@3 was 1.000. This case demonstrates that reranking can improve the ordering of relevant candidates when they are already present in the candidate pool.

### Case 2 — `genai-004`: self-attention

**Question:** What is self-attention?

After reranking, `generative_ai_reference.md::chunk_1` ranked first and provided a direct definition. However, the second labelled relevant chunk, `generative_ai_reference.md::chunk_0`, ranked seventh among the 10 candidates. Two chunks about decoder attention in the RAG implementation ranked second and third.

The resulting Precision@3 was 0.333 and Recall@3 was 0.500. The source text is split across adjacent chunks, with the first chunk ending mid-sentence. This suggests chunk boundaries and the reranker's preference for a complete definition may contribute to the missed chunk. This case remains a failure at the Top-3 cutoff.

### Case 3 — `rag-005`: updating RAG knowledge

**Question:** How can a RAG knowledge source be updated without retraining the language model?

The reranker selected three labelled relevant chunks:
1. `rag_reference.md::chunk_0`
2. `rag_documentation.md::chunk_6`
3. `rag_documentation.md::chunk_5`

Precision@3, Recall@3, and MRR were all 1.000. This is a successful case. However, chunks 5 and 6 overlap in content, so the metrics reward both according to the current labels even though they may provide partly redundant evidence.

## Reproducing the experiments

Run commands from the project root with the virtual environment activated.

### 1. Install dependencies

```powershell
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
```

### 2. Run the test suite

```powershell
pytest -v
```

The project previously passed with 33 tests. Confirm the current result before publishing.

### 3. Run the audited baseline

```powershell
python -m src.cli --benchmark data/benchmark_audited_v1.json --top-k 5 --output-dir reports/audited_baseline_v1
```

### 4. Run the Top-K experiments

```powershell
python -m src.cli --benchmark data/benchmark_audited_v1.json --top-k 3 --output-dir reports/topk_experiment/k3
python -m src.cli --benchmark data/benchmark_audited_v1.json --top-k 5 --output-dir reports/topk_experiment/k5
python -m src.cli --benchmark data/benchmark_audited_v1.json --top-k 10 --output-dir reports/topk_experiment/k10
```

### 5. Run the reranking experiment

```powershell
python -m src.evaluation.rerank_experiment --benchmark data/benchmark_audited_v1.json --candidate-k 10 --top-k 3 --output-dir reports/reranking_experiment
```

The first run may download the embedding and cross-encoder model weights. A Hugging Face token is not required for public models under ordinary rate limits. On Windows, a symlink-cache warning may appear; the download can still work, though it may use more disk space.

### 6. Inspect the saved reports

Reports are timestamped JSON files under their respective output directories. Preserve existing reports so the original baseline and subsequent experiments remain traceable.

## Limitations and next steps

- The benchmark contains 25 questions and is relatively small; results may not generalize to other document collections or query types.
- Relevance labels were manually reviewed, not independently double-annotated.
- The evaluation measures retrieval relevance, not answer correctness, faithfulness, hallucination rate, or end-to-end user satisfaction.
- The cross-encoder reranks only the top 10 candidates. It cannot recover relevant chunks that candidate retrieval fails to include.
- Overlapping chunks may count as multiple relevant results and affect precision/recall interpretation.
- Cross-encoder inference adds latency and resource cost; the current report does not include a latency benchmark.
- The chunking process produces some awkward splits and mid-sentence boundaries. A future experiment could compare alternative chunk sizes/overlap using a separate, isolated vector database and a newly generated index.
- Consider adding answer-level evaluation, latency measurements, confidence intervals, and a larger independently reviewed benchmark in future iterations.
